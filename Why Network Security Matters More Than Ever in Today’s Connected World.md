# Why Network Security Matters More Than Ever in Today’s Connected World\!

Organizations and businesses around the world are highly reliant on their networks for conducting everyday operations such as online banking, storing data in the cloud, communicating with co-workers and customers, and managing student records. Interconnected networks are in a constant state of transmitting information. As convenient and efficient as this connectivity is, it creates a tremendous amount of risk related to security. Cybercriminals constantly seek ways to take advantage of network vulnerabilities, which is why network security is so important to contemporary cybersecurity programs.

Network security is defined as a combination of policies, technologies, and procedures that are used to prevent unauthorized access to a computer network or misuse, modification or interruption of the operation of a computer network. The primary objective of network security is to ensure that data is transmitted across the network securely; confidentially; integrity; and only to authorized users. Companies use network security to protect confidential information such as financial records, personal information and intellectual property.

## **Basics of Network Security**

The essence of network security is to protect the communication infrastructure that connects devices such as computers, servers, routers and mobile devices. The network can provide means for these devices to exchange information with one another but they must have protections in place against malicious activities.

Network security is based on three key principles:

Confidentiality is the principle by which sensitive information is provided only to authorized users and is protected from being accessed by unauthenticated parties through the use of encryption technologies.

Integrity involves ensuring that data being transferred between two or more parties is not modified or altered in any way during transit. Mechanisms for providing integrity include: algorithms which can verify that data received is the same as the data that was sent.

Availability involves allowing users to access resources and services that are provided by a network at the time they are needed. In order for users to be able to access the resources and services provided by a network, the network must be able to withstand disruptions due to attacks from unauthorized sources, as well as have sufficient failings to prevent the loss of resources through mechanical problems.

The maintenance of these three principles will assist organizations in creating a secure and reliable digital environment.

## **Technologies to Secure Networks**

Multiple technologies secure networks and enable effective monitoring of network activity.

The commonly used and essential security technologies are firewalls, intrusion detection systems (IDS), intrusion prevention systems (IPS), and virtual private networks (VPNs).

Firewalls are the first line of defence between an internal, trusted network and an external network such as the Internet. Firewalls monitor and filter all incoming and outgoing network traffic and apply security rules that determine whether traffic is to be allowed through or blocked. Firewalls enable blocking many of the most common types of cyberattacks by filtering out the majority of potentially suspicious or unauthorised traffic.

Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS) are designed to monitor network traffic for unusual patterns or activity that could indicate a potential or ongoing intrusion. When an attempt at intruding into a network is detected, IDS systems alert the system administrator so that an appropriate response may be taken. IDS systems are particularly useful for identifying unusual behaviour that may suggest a security breach may occur.

Intrusion Prevention Systems (IPS) are similar to IDS systems, but they actively prevent threats from being executed by blocking suspicious network activity before it reaches its intended destination. IPS systems are, therefore, an active form of network defence; IDS systems function solely as monitoring tools.

Virtual Private Networks (VPNs) enable users to engage in a secure method of communicating using the internet as their medium, by encrypting all communication prior to sending it, so that even if a third party was able to intercept it, they would have difficulty reading this information in plain text. VPNs have become very popular amongst businesses to allow remote workers access to company systems and data, while maintaining security.

These technologies (VPNs and encryption) provide multiple layers of security which create a strong defense from cyber attacks for the corporate network.

## **Common Network Attacks**

Despite implementing security protocols; a cyber criminal has the ability to launch an attack on a network. Understanding the types of attacks that are common within networks is important when establishing effective methods of prevention.

The most common attack is called "Man in the Middle" (MITM) attack which is where an assailant can spy upon communications between two users that think they are communicating directly. As an example, an MITM would capture their user ID and password when transmitted over an unsecured wireless network and other sensitive information without the original parties knowing. The majority of MITM attacks are done remotely via an unprotected Wi-Fi connection.

Another well-known threat is known as packet sniffing which is capturing and analyzing data packets as they traverse through a network, while packet sniffing programs can be utilized to troubleshoot a network by an administrator, malicious individuals may utilize packet sniffing programs to capture sensitive information such as a user's ID or password as well as any personal identifying information if that data was not properly encrypted.

DDoS (Distributed Denial of Service) attacks are one of the most disruptive kinds of attacks. A DDoS attack utilizes numerous compromised devices (commonly referred to as botnets) to create large amounts of traffic destined for a target server or network. These attacks overwhelm the targeted system's resources, rendering it unavailable to provide legitimate users with access to the services offered by that system. DDoS attacks can result in significant interruption of online services, web sites, and business operations.

## **The future of Network Security** 

As the Internet becomes more interconnected with devices and services, the rate of evolution of Cyber Threats continues to increase, thus the need of organisations continues to be very much on High Alert level with respect to securing their Networks against Amateurs, Hackers, Data Breaches, Denial of Services (DoS).

Organisations can significantly mitigate their risk of Cyber Attacks by implementing Strong Security Technologies (e.g. Firewalls, IDS/IPS Systems; VPN’s) and adhering to Secure Best Practices.

When we talk about Network Security, it is not only a technical matter but also one of an organisation's fundamental obligations to maintain Trust with its customers; also to protect Sensitive Information; also to maintain Today's Digital Systems’ reliability.

To maintain reliable operations of Consumer Operations and safeguard all of the Users who rely upon them, both organisations must invest in Reliable Network Security.
